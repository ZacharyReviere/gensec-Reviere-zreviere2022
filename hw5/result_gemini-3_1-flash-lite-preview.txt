#### Definition: Ransomware
Ransomware is a form of malware designed to encrypt files on a device, rendering them and the systems that rely on them unusable. Malicious actors then demand ransom in exchange for decryption. Modern tactics often include "double extortion," where data is exfiltrated and threatened to be released publicly if the ransom is not paid.

#### CISA Guidance Summary
According to the [CISA #StopRansomware Guide](https://www.cisa.gov/stopransomware/ransomware-guide), organizations should focus on prevention through robust security hygiene. The guide highlights the need to defend against common initial access vectors, implement zero trust architecture, and maintain secure, offline backups to ensure recovery capability without paying ransoms.

#### Prioritized Mitigations
1.  **Disable SMB Exposure:** Ensure that port 445 is not exposed to the public internet. Use firewalls to restrict access to authorized internal segments only.
2.  **Patch Management:** Maintain up-to-date systems to protect against known SMB vulnerabilities (e.g., EternalBlue).
3.  **Network Segmentation:** Implement network segmentation to limit the ability of attackers to move laterally from a compromised workstation or server.
4.  **Access Control:** Enforce the principle of least privilege; disable SMBv1, which is obsolete and insecure, in favor of current versions.
5.  **Backup Strategy:** Follow the 3-2-1 backup rule—keep three copies of data on two different media types, with one copy kept offsite and disconnected from the network.
